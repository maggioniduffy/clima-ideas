Learning resources: streamflow models of F2.5 (GR4J + CemaNeige, LSTM, metrics, data)

1. Rainfall-runoff modelling (the background for everything)

Keith Beven, Rainfall-Runoff Modelling: The Primer (Wiley). It explains what conceptual models like GR4J do, calibration, equifinality, and why "good NSE" can mislead.

2. GR4J and CemaNeige (the baseline)

Perrin, Michel & Andréassian (2003), J. Hydrol. 279: the GR4J paper, short and readable.
Valéry, Andréassian & Perrin (2014), J. Hydrol. 517: CemaNeige, the snow module.
The airGR R package (INRAE) and its vignettes: the reference implementation, with diagrams of the stores. It's the best way to see the model "move".

3. LSTMs in general

Christopher Olah, Understanding LSTM Networks (colah's blog, 2015): the classic intuitive explanation with diagrams.
Dive into Deep Learning (d2l.ai), the RNN and LSTM chapters: free, with runnable code.

4. LSTMs for streamflow (what we're doing)

Kratzert et al. (2018), HESS, "Rainfall–runoff modelling using Long Short-Term Memory networks": the founding paper.
Kratzert et al. (2019), HESS, "Towards learning universal, regional, and local hydrological behaviors…": training on many basins, static attributes and the NSE* loss we use. This is the most directly relevant one.
Nearing et al. (2021), Water Resources Research, "What role does hydrological science play in the age of machine learning?": the debate, ML vs. conceptual models.
NeuralHydrology (Python library and its documentation/tutorials): the reference implementation of these papers. It's well worth comparing against our lstm.py.

5. Metrics

Nash & Sutcliffe (1970), J. Hydrol. 10: NSE.
Gupta et al. (2009), J. Hydrol. 377: KGE and its decomposition.
Knoben et al. (2019), HESS, technical note "Inherent benchmark or not? Comparing NSE and KGE": why the two aren't comparable on the same scale. It explains why our results disagree between NSE and KGE.

6. Data

Addor et al. (2017), HESS: CAMELS, the idea of large-sample datasets.
Alvarez-Garreton et al. (2018), HESS 22: CAMELS-CL, the one we pretrain on.
Kratzert et al. (2023), Scientific Data: Caravan.
Oudin et al. (2005), J. Hydrol.: the PET formula we use.

Suggested route: Olah's post, then Kratzert 2018, then Kratzert 2019, then the NeuralHydrology tutorials. For the hydrology side: the Perrin 2003 paper, then airGR, then Beven's book.

In this repo: backend/src/clima_nqn/stages/flood/hydro_models.py and lstm.py; their opening docstrings explain each choice and cite these sources.
